# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Enterprise ops and IT teams at larger organizations managing complex, multi-system environments. These are people juggling CRMs, finance tools, support platforms, databases, and documents simultaneously. They work in teams, hand off context to colleagues, and need the system to be reliable enough to trust with actual decisions. They are not developers, but they are not technophobes either; they are pragmatic professionals who have been burned by fragmentation and want it to stop.

## Product Purpose

Cortex is an AI business operating system: a unified workspace where enterprise ops teams connect their scattered business systems, ask natural-language questions across all of them, generate reports, and automate workflows through AI agents. Success looks like a user ending a session feeling that their data, team, and tools finally exist in one coherent place rather than five disconnected tabs.

## Brand Personality

Warm, precise, connective. The voice is confident without being cold. It sounds like the smartest person in the room who is also genuinely interested in helping you succeed, not just demonstrating their intelligence. Calm under complexity. Never hype-y.

## Anti-references

- **Generic SaaS dashboard**: white cards on white backgrounds, blue primary buttons, identical icon + heading + text card grids, hero-metric templates (big number, small label, gradient accent). If it looks like a Figma community template, it has failed.
- **AI hype aesthetic**: purple-to-blue gradients, neural network particle backgrounds, glowing orbs, "powered by AI" banners everywhere, futuristic chrome. Cortex is intelligent; it does not need to announce that loudly.

## Design Principles

1. **Unity over isolation.** Every surface should reinforce that all the user's systems are now one. Visual continuity, shared language, and coherent navigation across integrations are not polish; they are the product.
2. **Confident warmth.** Enterprise-grade capability without enterprise coldness. The UI should feel like a trusted colleague, not a procurement portal.
3. **Show, don't announce.** Intelligence is demonstrated through what the product does, not labeled with it. No "AI-powered" badges on every feature.
4. **Earn trust through clarity.** If a user cannot understand what is happening or why, the product has failed. Transparent state, honest empty states, clear errors.
5. **The whole stays visible.** Users should feel the scope of their connected environment at a glance, without needing to drill into five separate views to get orientation.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA. Keyboard navigation throughout, sufficient color contrast for all text and interactive elements, screen reader compatible components. Theme toggle (dark/light) already implemented; ensure both themes meet AA contrast requirements.
